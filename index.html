The SOL way

This house runs on sunlight.

SOL stands for Sustainable + Opulent + Living, and the first word isn’t decoration. Over a year, the 42 solar panels in the meadow are sized to make about 140% of the power the house uses, sauna, hot tub and heated floors included. You don’t give up a single comfort to travel lighter.

How SOL works →
What it means for your stay

Nothing rationed

Run the sauna, soak in the hot tub, leave the floors warm. Over a year the sun covers it, so there’s no meter to watch and nothing to feel bad about.

The lights stay on

Three Tesla Powerwalls hold 40.5 kWh. When a storm takes the lines down, the essentials keep running on stored sunlight.

Arrive electric, leave full

A Level 2 charger by the garage, free for guests.

“…charged fully in only a handful of hours.”

Daichi

Water from 600 feet down

A private well inside the New York City watershed, the protected land that fills the city’s reservoirs.

14 tonnes

of CO₂ kept out of the air each year: the same as burning about 1,600 gallons of gasoline, or what 240 tree seedlings take in over ten years of growing.

That’s enough electricity for about two average American homes.

The installer’s estimate of 21,185 kWh a year from the 42-panel, 17.85 kW array, converted at the EPA’s national rates (Greenhouse Gas Equivalencies Calculator); average home use from the U.S. EIA. Upstate New York’s grid is already cleaner than average, so the local offset is smaller. Real output varies with the weather.
